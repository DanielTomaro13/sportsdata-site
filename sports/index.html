What the sports board shows

A live value terminal for team sports. It treats the prediction markets and the betting exchange as the sharp line — the closest thing to a true probability the public market offers — then measures every bookmaker against it and shows you where the books disagree.

The board covers the major codes carried by the sportsdata data plane: AFL, NRL, NBA, MLB, NFL, NHL and the European football leagues, alongside the markets quoted for them. Prices come from Kalshi, Polymarket, Betfair and Pinnacle on the sharp side, and the retail books on the other.

How to read it

The sharp line

The sharp price is a de-vigged blend of whichever of Kalshi, Polymarket, Betfair and Pinnacle have priced that game. Those four are grouped together for a reason: two are prediction markets where traders take a position with their own money, one is an exchange where punters bet against each other, and Pinnacle is the low-margin book that welcomes winning players. All four are disciplined by money in a way a retail promotional price is not.

De-vigging matters. A bookmaker's quoted odds include their margin, so the implied probabilities across a market always sum to more than 100%. Stripping that margin out and re-normalising is what turns a price into a probability you can actually compare.

Value

Value is the gap between the best price any book is offering and the sharp line's own fair price for the same outcome. A positive edge means one book is longer than the sharp market implies. It is a statement about disagreement between markets, not a prediction about the game.

Same-game multis

The SGM generator prices a same-game multi as a correlated combination rather than by naively multiplying the legs together. Legs within one game are not independent — a team winning big makes its own players' overs more likely — and pricing that correlation properly is the difference between a real number and a guess. The engine prices the combination, so you can compare it against the bookmaker's offer.

Running it live

The public board animates a captured sequence of real market data so it works for everyone without a backend; the sharp line, the industry ladders and the SGM generator all function. Point it at your own warehouse-backed instance and it polls live feeds instead.
