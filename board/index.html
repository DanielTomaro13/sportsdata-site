What the racing board shows

A live money-flow terminal for Australian thoroughbred, harness and greyhound racing. It watches where money is actually going — tote pool share and bookmaker price moves — prices every runner itself, and marks the runners the market has not caught up with yet.

Everything on the board is derived from real market data pulled through the sportsdata data plane: Sportsbet and PointsBet for bookmaker prices, Betfair for exchange money, and tote pool figures for where the cash is landing. Nothing here is a tip service — the board shows you the market's own behaviour and its own inconsistencies.

How to read it

Money in

Money in means a runner's share of the tote pool is rising while its price shortens. That is the clearest public signal that money is arriving for a horse, and it is what the scrolling tape at the top of the board tracks. A runner marked with multiple ticks has firmed across more than one market, which filters out the noise of a single book adjusting.

Fair price

The fair column is the board's own price for each runner, built by removing the bookmaker's margin (the overround) from the market and re-normalising. Comparing fair against the best available price across the books is how the board finds value: when the best price on offer is longer than the fair price, that runner is an overlay.

Value and overlays

The value panel lists those overlays, ranked by the size of the edge. An overlay is not a prediction that a runner will win — it is a statement that one book is offering a longer price than the rest of the market implies, which is a very different and much more measurable claim.

Scorecard

The scorecard keeps the board honest. It replays every pick the board has made against the actual results and reports strike rate, return on investment and profit or loss at flat stakes. If the method is not working, the scorecard is where you will see it first.

Generating a price

The price generator at the foot of the race panel prices exotics and same-race multis off the board's own fair prices rather than off the bookmaker's, so you can see what a combination should cost before you compare it to what is being offered.

Running it live

The public board animates a captured sequence of real market data so the page works for everyone without a backend. Run it against your own machine and it polls the live feeds instead — the source is on GitHub, and the underlying feeds are the same free MCP tools the rest of the site is built on. See also the sports board, which applies the same idea to team sports, and the agent roster if you would rather ask questions than read a terminal.
